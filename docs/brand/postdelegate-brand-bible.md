## 1. Brand core

### Name

**PostDelegate**

Pronunciation: **POST DEL-uh-gayt** (delegate, the verb)

Arabic companion: **بوست ديليجيت**

PostDelegate combines *post* and *delegate*: give a trusted team or agent the
publishing work while keeping control over approval and delivery. Always write
the product name as `PostDelegate` in prose and `postdelegate` in the lowercase
wordmark or identifiers that require lowercase. Existing internal namespaces
remain unchanged.

PostDelegate replaces the previous Orbiloom customer-facing name. The earlier
Orbiloom specifications remain historical records; this document is the current
source for the product identity. The name does not establish domain ownership
or a live hosted service. Use `https://postdelegate.example` in unconfigured
examples until a production origin is configured.

### Purpose

Give every team and trusted agent a dependable way to create, coordinate,
approve and publish across social channels.

### Promise

Create once, coordinate clearly and publish across your connected channels
from one governed system.

### Category

Agentic social publishing infrastructure.

### Primary value proposition

> Social publishing infrastructure for humans and AI agents.

### Campaign line

> Delegate the work. Keep control.

### Hero line

> Put every channel in motion.

### Arabic core lines

- Primary value proposition: **بنية تحتية للنشر الاجتماعي للبشر ووكلاء الذكاء الاصطناعي**
- Campaign line: **فوّض العمل. واحتفظ بالتحكم.**
- Hero line: **حرّك كل قناة في مسار واحد.**

Arabic is rewritten for natural rhythm and meaning. It is not forced to mirror
English word order.

## 2. Audience

PostDelegate serves these audiences worldwide. Position the product around
customer needs and publishing workflows, without making any country or region
the default market. Language support is a product capability.

### Creators

They need a clear calendar, fast creation, reliable scheduling and useful
analytics without becoming social-operations specialists.

### Social teams and agencies

They need collaboration, client separation, approvals, brand consistency and a
record of who changed or published what.

### Developers and automation teams

They need stable interfaces, deterministic outputs, scoped credentials,
idempotent mutations and documentation that matches the real product.

### AI-agent builders

They need structured tools that let agents draft, request approval, schedule
and inspect status under the same policy and audit trail as people.

No audience is framed as more legitimate than another. The workspace and
programmatic interfaces are entry points into the same publishing system.

## 3. Voice attributes (confidence: High)

| We are | We are not |
| --- | --- |
| **Clear** — outcomes and limits are easy to understand | **Vague** — padded with abstract promises |
| **Capable** — technically credible and operationally calm | **Needlessly technical** — showing complexity for status |
| **Kinetic** — language carries purposeful motion | **Frenetic** — loud, rushed or childish |
| **Inclusive** — people and agents both belong | **Robot-centric** — treating humans as legacy users |
| **Globally aware** — localization is deep and contextual | **Culturally generic** — pretending every market communicates alike |
| **Candid** — shipped, preview and planned states are distinct | **Speculative** — presenting roadmap ideas as available |

### Clear

- What it means: the reader knows what PostDelegate does after one sentence.
- How it shows up: active verbs, short headings, explicit status labels.
- Avoid: stacked nouns, vague “solutions”, unexplained acronyms.
- Evidence: owner-approved global position and interface-parity requirement.

### Capable

- What it means: PostDelegate understands both creative work and dependable
  infrastructure.
- How it shows up: connect features to real workflow behavior and controls.
- Avoid: talking down to creators or using code terminology as decoration.
- Evidence: Postiz engine, approval system and first-class interface direction.

### Kinetic

- What it means: the brand feels coordinated and in motion.
- How it shows up: create, connect, move, schedule, publish, measure.
- Avoid: urgency theater, excessive exclamation marks, animation everywhere.
- Evidence: retained connected-workflow visual language and coordinated
  publishing behavior.

### Inclusive

- What it means: humans keep control while agents gain legitimate operating
  interfaces.
- How it shows up: “humans and AI agents”, “your team and trusted agents”.
- Avoid: replacement narratives and anthropomorphic robot clichés.
- Evidence: explicit agent-first positioning with human approval controls.

### Globally aware

- What it means: English is a default, not a cultural center.
- How it shows up: localization is specific—Arabic, RTL, dialect and campaign
  calendars—without presenting one region as the whole market.
- Avoid: flags as language selectors, literal Arabic translation, MENA-first
  category language.
- Evidence: approved global-first direction and retained localization depth.

### Candid

- What it means: confidence is earned through accurate claims.
- How it shows up: “available”, “preview” and “planned” mean different things.
- Avoid: invented prices, customer counts, testimonials or interface parity.
- Evidence: owner direction and open-source/source-offer requirements.

## 5. Messaging framework (confidence: High)

### Pillar 1: One governed publishing system

- Core idea: every interface reaches the same organizations, channels, drafts,
  approvals and publishing pipeline.
- Use when: explaining product architecture, collaboration or reliability.
- Example: “Plan in the workspace. Automate through MCP. Approve once. Publish
  through the same system.”

### Pillar 2: Agent-ready by design

- Core idea: agents are real operators with scoped access and accountable
  actions, not a chat widget attached to a scheduler.
- Use when: speaking to developers, automation teams and AI-forward buyers.
- Example: “Give agents tools, not loopholes.”

### Pillar 3: Global from the first screen

- Core idea: PostDelegate is globally understandable while offering meaningful
  localization depth.
- Use when: explaining Arabic, RTL, dialect and regional-calendar capability.
- Example: “Global by default. Local when the message demands it.”

### Pillar 4: Open and extensible

- Core idea: PostDelegate is built on the open-source Postiz engine and exposes
  first-class integration surfaces.
- Use when: building developer trust and explaining source availability.
- Example: “Use the workspace, connect your stack or inspect the source.”

### Competitive positioning

- Versus basic schedulers: governed automation and interface parity, not merely
  a calendar with an AI caption button.
- Versus developer-only APIs: a complete workspace for the humans who plan,
  review and measure the work.
- Versus closed agent tools: open-source foundation, explicit permissions and a
  visible audit trail.
- Versus the status quo: one connected system replaces scattered drafts,
  scripts, approvals and channel logins.

Never claim a feature advantage that the shipped product cannot demonstrate.

## 6. Tone by context (confidence: Medium)

Tone is inferred from the approved personality; customer evidence has not yet
validated every context.

| Context | Formality | Energy | Technical depth | Principle |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Landing page | Medium | High | Low–medium | Earn attention, then prove the system |
| Product UI | Medium | Calm | Low | Make the next action unmistakable |
| Developer docs | Medium | Medium | High | Exact inputs, outputs and failure states |
| CLI | Low | Low | High | Concise, deterministic, script-safe |
| Social media | Low–medium | High | Low | One useful thought at a time |
| Support | Medium | Warm | Medium | Acknowledge impact, then give a path forward |
| Security incident | High | Low | High | Facts, scope, action and next update |
| Sales proposal | High | Medium | Medium–high | Workflow fit and operational value |

### Product UI

- Prefer `Schedule post` to `Execute scheduling operation`.
- Confirm consequential scope: `Schedule 4 posts to 3 channels?`
- Explain recovery: `The draft is safe. Reconnect LinkedIn to publish it.`
- Never blame the user for an integration failure.

### Developer docs

- Begin with a working path and state prerequisites.
- Show exact request/response examples and idempotency behavior.
- Distinguish experimental, preview and stable interfaces.
- Avoid marketing claims inside reference documentation.

### Social media

- Lead with an observation, workflow or product proof.
- Use at most one exclamation mark and only when genuine.
- Do not imitate current meme language merely to appear relevant.
- Example: “An agent that can draft but cannot request approval is still a
  demo. Publishing infrastructure needs both.”

### Support

- Start: `I can see the scheduled post did not publish.`
- Then: `Your draft and media are safe. The channel authorization expired.`
- Finish with the exact recovery action and what happens next.

## 7. Terminology

### Must-use terms

| Term | Usage | Instead of | Example |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Workspace | Browser product used by people and teams | Dashboard, portal | “Open the workspace to review the draft.” |
| Publishing system | Shared governed backend and workflow | AI engine | “Every interface uses the same publishing system.” |
| AI agent | First reference to an autonomous or assisted operator | Bot | “Connect an AI agent through MCP.” |
| Channel | A connected social destination | Account, network | “Select three channels.” |
| Interface | Web, API, MCP, SDK or CLI surface | Version | “Choose the interface that fits the work.” |
| Approval policy | Rules governing consequential publishing actions | Guardrails | “Set the approval policy for this workspace.” |

### Preferred terms

- **Draft** for unpublished content.
- **Schedule** for a planned publish time.
- **Publish** for the consequential external action.
- **Connect** for adding a channel or programmatic client.
- **Localization** for language, direction, dialect and calendar adaptation.
- **Source** or **source code** for AGPL availability.

### Avoid

| Term | Reason | Alternative |
| --- | --- | --- |
| AI-powered | Empty without a specific behavior | State what the agent does |
| Magic | Hides operational behavior | Automatic, generated, suggested |
| Omnichannel | Enterprise cliché and often inaccurate | Across your social channels |
| Bot | Reductive and ambiguous | AI agent, automation |
| Effortless | Dismisses real work | Faster, coordinated, in one place |
| MENA-first | Conflicts with global position | Global-first with deep localization |

### Never use as claims

- “Revolutionary” or “game-changing”.
- “Publish everywhere” if unsupported networks are implied; say “across your
  connected channels”.
- “Fully autonomous” unless the organization has explicitly enabled and the
  product has implemented that policy.
- Invented social proof, prices, rankings or testimonials.

## 9. Logo system

### Concept

Two interwoven paths connect people, agents, content and channels. A Signal
Lime node occupies the upper-right trajectory. The retained mark represents
connected publishing workflows and coordinated motion as an abstract
workflow symbol.

### Lockups

- Primary: mark + lowercase `postdelegate`.
- Arabic companion: mark + `بوست ديليجيت`.
- Mark-only: product icon, favicon, avatar and compact navigation.
- Monochrome: Cloud on dark or Void on light when color reproduction is not
  reliable.

### Clear space

Keep at least one signal-node diameter around every edge of the mark or lockup.

### Minimum size

- Mark: 16px digital; verify the signal node and crossing remain distinct.
- Horizontal lockup: 96px wide digital.
- Arabic lockup: 104px wide digital due to connected letterforms.

### Misuse

Do not rotate, stretch, add shadows, outline the wordmark, recolor individual
paths outside approved variants, place it on visual noise, mirror it in RTL, or
replace the node with a sparkle/robot/speech bubble.

The mark itself does not mirror in RTL. Lockup order follows the document
direction when constructed with live text in the application.

### Proportions

On dark marketing surfaces, use roughly 70% Void/Deep Space, 20% Cloud/Steel
content, 8% Violet and no more than 2% Lime/Coral accents. Product UI may use
less accent color to protect hierarchy.

### Accessibility

- Cloud on Void is the default text pair.
- Cloud is used on Orbit Violet buttons; verify each button size/weight.
- Void on Signal Lime is allowed for high-salience filled actions.
- Signal Lime is not body text on Cloud.
- Color never communicates state without text, icon or shape.

## 11. Typography

### Latin

**Manrope** serves display, body and product UI. It is direct and technical
without turning developer-only. Use weights 500, 600 and 700. Large marketing
headlines use tight tracking; product copy uses normal tracking.

### Arabic

**IBM Plex Sans Arabic** serves Arabic UI and marketing. Tune Arabic line-height
independently and avoid artificial Latin-style tracking. Use Arabic punctuation
and native phrasing.

### Technical

**JetBrains Mono** serves code, CLI commands, response payloads, MCP tools and
small interface labels. It is not a decorative body font.

### Scale

- Marketing display: `clamp(3.5rem, 9vw, 8.5rem)`, line-height 0.9–0.96.
- Section heading: `clamp(2rem, 5vw, 4.5rem)`, line-height 1.0.
- Body large: 18–22px, line-height 1.5.
- Product body: 14–16px, line-height 1.45–1.6.
- Technical label: 11–13px, uppercase only for short Latin labels.

## 12. Layout, imagery and motion

### Layout

Use generous negative space with precise modular surfaces. Lead with product
workflow previews: composing, calendar planning, approval and publishing status.
Connected paths support the workflow story without competing with the product.
Use logical CSS properties so RTL is supported throughout the layout.

### Graphic language

- Thin elliptical paths and woven crossings.
- Nodes that represent people, agents, drafts and channels.
- Modular panels resembling real product states.
- Restrained atmospheric violet fields.
- Product UI and real interface simulations over generic 3D decoration.

### Photography

Photography is optional. When used, show real creative/operational work with
natural light and editorial framing. Avoid staged headset teams, glowing robot
faces, holographic dashboards and generic hand-on-laptop stock imagery.

### Motion

Motion follows a path or communicates status. Nodes travel, content enters in a
coordinated sequence, and a successful signal pulses once. Avoid infinite
motion that competes with reading. Respect `prefers-reduced-motion` and keep all
core meaning available without animation.

## 13. Interface applications

### Marketing website

Use Void, clear Manrope headlines, truthful product workflow previews and
high-contrast CTAs. Show composing, calendar planning and approvals in a
connected sequence. Keep language and localization capabilities inside relevant
product explanations; they do not define a country-specific audience or require
a dedicated regional showcase. The Postiz reference informs information
density and clarity; keep the product identity, copy and visuals original.

### Product workspace

Protect task clarity. Use Violet for primary operations, Lime for rare live or
high-salience signals, Coral for alerts and Steel for secondary information.
Do not turn every control into brand decoration.

### API and MCP documentation

Pair plain-language workflow explanation with exact JetBrains Mono examples.
State auth scope, idempotency and errors alongside successful requests.

### CLI

The product name may appear in onboarding and help; command output remains
concise. JSON output contains no decorative language or ANSI color. Human output
uses color as enhancement, never as the only status cue.

### Social content

Use bold editorial statements, operational examples, original product visuals
and open-source proof. Avoid trend mimicry that weakens technical credibility.
